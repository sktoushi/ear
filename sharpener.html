Fatigue forecast: —
How the fatigue forecast decides a break
The SM-2 scheduler is untouched. This layer only watches review events and models the human: every graded turn adds strain = grade cost (again 2.4 · hard 1.5 · good 0.9 · easy 0.6) × time-on-task factor (up to +60% after ~45 min) × replay factor (+8% per extra loop pass on that segment). Autopilot rolls only count 35% — a machine roll doesn't tire you. Accumulated load decays passively while you idle, and drops sharply after a break in proportion to the seconds actually watched. Load is mapped to a 0–100% fatigue index through 100·(1−e^(−load/capacity)), where capacity comes from the sensitivity gauge.

The forecast is the index projected one turn ahead (load + EWMA strain per turn). When that projection crosses your trigger, the minimum-gap gauge is satisfied and a break video is loaded, the next card is held and the break video plays one slice; then playback hands straight back to the normal pickNext(). No break is ever inserted mid-card, so no card sees an altered interval.

The break slice defaults to exactly the learning slice length; the multiplier gauge (0.25×–4×) makes it shorter or longer. The break video resumes where the previous break stopped and wraps around at the end. Keys: k break now / end break · Esc end break. Inbound URL params: bv, bmul, bspeed, bthresh, bgap, bsens.

How the pre-emptive switch protects the flow
Every segment you actually finish a loop pass on is registered as warm — it has just been decoded, so the player very likely still holds it. The shield watches two signals at 200 ms resolution: (1) a hard stall — getCurrentTime() stops advancing while the state is PLAYING/BUFFERING for longer than your grace window — and (2) a pre-emptive signal — getVideoLoadedFraction() projected against the playhead shows less than your headroom of buffer left, i.e. a stall is about to happen. Either one fires the hop.

On a hop, the current card is held, never graded and never re-scheduled: SM-2 state, EF, interval and due-step are all frozen exactly as they were. The player jumps to the most recently warmed segment (preferring one that still sits inside the reported buffered range), loops it, disables the grade buttons and holds autopilot — so the room stays full of the target language instead of a spinner. After the retry delay the shield seeks back to the real card and probes it for 2.5 s; if it stalls again the hold doubles (exponential backoff, capped at 60 s) and the filler resumes. A clean probe resets the backoff and hands straight back to the untouched playCurrent().

The same logic guards the break video: completed break slices are remembered, and a stalling break jumps to a previous slice rather than freezing your rest. Because the review player is paused during a break, it quietly buffers ahead the whole time — breaks and the shield reinforce each other. Keys: c toggle shield · Esc force-return to the real segment. URL params: cs, cgrace, chead, cbreak.

How scheduling, auto mode & infinite reset work
Each segment is a card. Grading runs SM-2 (Anki), updating ease factor, reps and interval; intervals are mapped to steps ahead in the queue, so Again/Hard resurface soon and Good/Easy get pushed back until they graduate. State is saved to IndexedDB keyed by video + segment length, so a reload resumes exactly where you left off.

The mastery signal blends learned fraction, mean ease factor, and recent retention. When it crosses the threshold (comprehension high), progress auto-resets — SRS state clears, the round counter increments, and drilling continues infinitely. Uncheck Infinite auto-reset to stop at completion instead.

Auto mode waits for the configured number of loop passes on the current segment, then rolls a grade from the chosen bias distribution and submits it, moving straight to the next card — hands-free, endlessly. A pending roll is cancelled the instant you press a grade key/button, pause playback (autopilot holds while paused), undo, or untick the box; in all those cases the original manual flow prevails. Autopilot grades still drive SM-2 (otherwise nothing would advance) but are excluded from the Retention stat and the mastery retention window, and each card shows a ⚙ pill counting how many of its reviews were machine-rolled.
